# Choosing prediction sources and supporting evidence

MoSelecta integrates coding-gene predictions using model quality, structural agreement between prediction sources, and optional protein and transcript evidence. Because cross-source support contributes to scoring and can influence pruning decisions, it is important to consider **how independently the input annotations were generated**, rather than simply how many files are supplied.

These are recommendations for interpreting and preparing inputs, not restrictions imposed by MoSelecta.

## Prefer complementary prediction sources

Where possible, use genome-wide coding-gene predictions generated by complementary methods or workflows on the **same target genome assembly**. Different files are not necessarily independent sources of support:

- A prediction set that is largely a subset or copy of another provides little additional independent corroboration.
- Repeated runs of the same predictor with only minor parameter changes may generate highly correlated models.
- Different tools can also share training data, hints, reference annotations, or other upstream information.

Such predictions can still contain useful alternatives, but treating each closely related set as a separate source may overstate the strength of cross-source agreement. Consider consolidating highly related sets under one input source when their provenance and dependence justify doing so.

## Grouping projected annotations

This is particularly relevant to large collections of gene models projected from related genomes. For example, projections from several cultivars or clones of the same species may be more appropriately represented as **one source** than as many nominally independent sources. Consolidating projections from related species, including species within the same genus, may also be useful when they share reference annotations or projection methods.

Grouping should follow **the origin and dependence of the predictions**, not taxonomy alone. Projections based on distinct reference annotations or genuinely different methods may offer complementary information and warrant separate source labels.

When consolidating GFF3/GTF files, preserve target-genome coordinates, strand, feature hierarchy, CDS phases, and unique gene/transcript identifiers. Resolve duplicate IDs and redundant models as appropriate; simple file concatenation may not be sufficient. Combining related predictions into one source avoids assigning them separate cross-source support, but does not make duplicate models or other input-quality issues disappear.

## Reusing protein and transcript evidence

Protein or transcript alignments used to guide evidence-based predictors such as **BRAKER** or **GALBA** can also be supplied directly to MoSelecta. The predictor's gene models are inferred coding structures rather than necessarily direct copies of individual alignments, and direct evidence may still help MoSelecta discriminate between competing structures. However, the resulting predictions and reused alignments **are not independent biological observations**; their agreement should be interpreted accordingly.

More caution is warranted when coding models are **derived directly from the same transcript assemblies** subsequently provided as `--evidence-transcripts`. For example, CDS models reconstructed from StringTie assemblies could be reinforced by reusing those exact assemblies as transcript evidence. In this situation, consider using independent transcript evidence, or omitting the duplicated evidence when it adds little independent information. The appropriate choice depends on how the candidate models were generated and what the alignments contribute.

## Practical interpretation

MoSelecta does not require every input to be independent, and models supported by only one source may still be retained under its scoring, evidence, and pruning rules. In particular, **support from two distinct input labels is not necessarily two independent validations**. The aim is to provide informative, complementary candidate structures rather than maximise the number of nominal sources.

There is no universally optimal number of prediction sources. Additional sources may improve gene recovery but can also introduce redundant or lower-confidence models. Choose inputs with regard to their provenance, complementarity, and the desired balance between sensitivity and precision.
